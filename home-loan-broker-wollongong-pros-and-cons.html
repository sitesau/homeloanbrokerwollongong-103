Questions That Shape Recommendations

A productive broker discussion should result in clear, understandable reasons for any loan recommendation. Australian mortgage brokers are legally obligated to act in the borrower's best interests and must explain the suitability of any recommended loan. It is important to ask what options were compared, which were ruled out, and what factors could alter the recommendation. For refinancing, queries should extend to the total cost, loan features, any discharge or setup fees, and the rationale behind debt consolidation. For investment loans, understanding how the loan structure aligns with the overall portfolio, the implications of interest-only repayments, and the lender's assessment process is vital. Self-employed borrowers should clarify whether full documentation, alternative documentation, or low documentation pathways best match their available evidence. Construction loan applicants should inquire about progress payment schedules, valuation timelines, builder documentation requirements, and how lenders handle house and land packages. Bridging loan borrowers need to understand contingency plans should their existing property sell later than anticipated.

Broker Comparison Versus Going Direct

Approaching a bank directly can be a straightforward process if the borrower has a clear preference for that bank's products and meets its lending criteria. However, this approach limits the range of options available. As noted, a bank will typically only offer its own products, whereas a mortgage broker can compare loans from multiple lenders, providing a broader perspective on suitability. Resources like ASIC Moneysmart offer valuable background information on home loan costs, features, and repayment obligations, helping borrowers make informed decisions before committing to a loan. A broker's guidance should leave the borrower with a clear understanding of repayment types, associated fees, loan conditions, the primary loan purpose, and the specific reasons why a particular option was favoured. This distinction becomes particularly important for complex lending scenarios, such as those involving variable income, construction loans, intricate investment structures, debt consolidation, or situations where a property needs to be sold.

  1. Define Your Loan Goal. Clearly state whether your objective is buying, refinancing, investing, building, bridging, or debt consolidation. This sets the direction for the broker.
  2. Summarise Your Income. Prepare details of your income, whether it's PAYG, contractor, ABN holder, business income, or a combination of household incomes.
  3. Outline Property Context. Describe the property situation: an established home, off-plan purchase, new build, house and land package, or refinancing an existing property.
  4. Note Your Constraints. Identify any limitations, such as deposit size, available income evidence, existing debts, a property to sell, a building contract, or uncertainty about repayment structures (fixed, variable, interest-only, P&I).
  5. Ask About Broker Remuneration. Inquire how the broker is paid, if any fees apply to you, and when this will be disclosed in writing. This helps clarify potential conflicts of interest.

Common questions

Is it better to use a mortgage broker or go directly to a bank for a home loan? A mortgage broker can compare loans from multiple lenders to find a product that suits your situation, while a bank offers only its own products. Brokers in Australia must act in your best interests and explain loan suitability. Many borrowers use brokers to save time and improve approval chances, offering broader choice and guidance compared to dealing with a single lender.

Do mortgage brokers in Wollongong charge fees or are they paid by the bank? Most Australian mortgage brokers receive a commission from the lender and do not charge a direct fee to standard home loan customers. However, some may charge fees for complex lending or specialised advice, which should be clearly disclosed upfront. It is advisable to ask a Wollongong broker how they are paid and if any fees apply to your specific situation.

What information should I prepare before enquiring with a home loan broker? Before enquiring, prepare details about your loan purpose (buying, refinancing, investing, building), your property plan, preferred timing, and any constraints such as deposit size, income evidence, or existing debts. Also, note your income type and property context. Asking how the broker is paid is also recommended.
